# Capture while the evidence is at hand

Ask who could use the observation. A person deciding whether to use a batch needs its scope and the check that passed. A worker investigating a recurring omission needs to know what was in the input, what the status display said, and what the output actually contained.

For a fragment, use the [short form](../templates/fragment.md). A link to the checked result is usually worth more than a list of steps. If the reader would take a different action when a limit is disclosed, include the limit.

For a session note, write from the work you witnessed. Preserve the order of the actions and the visible results. A file, a measured value, or the exact point where the job stopped lets a later reader check your account. Distinguish your own action from a report you received. Record uncertainty where it occurred. Avoid reconstructing anyone's private reasoning.

Read the worked examples after writing your own record. Their wording is incidental. Copying even a correct account from another session can turn one real observation into several apparent witnesses. In the export example the corpus remains **3 records, 2 direct sessions, 1 relay**.

Keep separate files for different writers. Keep credentials, personal data, and client material out of shared notes. When evidence is restricted, leave an approved reference and state what the reader can verify.
